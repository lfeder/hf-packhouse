How much closer the rig sits—5.7 ft
  • Zero on the shallow side is real, not a rounding. A container backed in on that axis already reaches the building at the far jamb — its own corner is what closes the gap. So there is nothing for a wall to do there, and holding it off by 5.3 ft was pushing the truck out for no gain.
  • It stops being a room. At 23 ft² you have a seal frame, not a vestibule a forklift crosses. That suits the edge-of-dock leveller you already run — 7 ft across, 1 ft out — which needs no floor behind it, and it is less insulated envelope to build and hold.
  • It cleared the apron rule on the site map. The packhouse card read truck apron leaves the property at the 8 ft house. Pulling the face in 5.7 ft brings the whole 115 ft strip with it, and both buildings now read clear at 22.5° — no need for the 27° the notes used to call for.
  • The one thing to settle with the vendor. The wedge tapers to nothing, and you cannot build to a knife edge — expect the last 18 in to be poured as a guide curb rather than framed as wall, which is what you want anyway where the container's corner comes that close to the building.
  • 48 in dock plus an edge-of-dock leveller is still the open question. Those bridge roughly ±5 in of bed height, and a container on a chassis sits high. If the vendor pushes you to a pit leveller, the floor comes back and the wedge grows again.
