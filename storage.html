How these are worked out

  • Cold storage need is every regular shipment staged in the cooler at once: one 40 ft container of cucumbers at 20 pallets (single-stacked 40 × 48 in pallets), a few pallets in the lettuce container (taken as 3), 8 pallets to Costco Kona and 5 in the box truck to Kawaihae. If it is two containers, need at +50% rises to 84.
  • Supplies need is today's on-hand pallets × 1.5. Burn goes up by half, so holding half again as much stock keeps the same weeks of cover you carry today.
  • Trays and film stay in the lettuce packhouse and are not counted here.
  • Months on hand is today's on-hand ÷ burn at +50%. Max storage shares the room's pallet positions out in proportion to how much of each item is stocked today, and months at max is that ÷ burn at +50%. Reslotting toward the fast movers (Keiki boxes, calcium nitrate) evens the months out.
  • Burn and on-hand come from the inventory system (weekly burn per item, last counts dated 2026-08-23 to 2026-09-14). A month is 4.345 weeks. +50% is applied to every item alike.
  • Pallet sizes are each item's own pallet or order unit in the system, e.g. 2,420 lb of calcium nitrate, 500 Keiki boxes, 64 cases of 1,000 bags.
  • Dry storage is racked two high on the floor plus one shelf level: three pallets per position. Wide: 8 rack lines × 6 bays × 2 pallets × 3 = 288. Narrow: 6 lines × 11 bays × 2 × 3 = 396.
  • Fert room is block-stacked two high: 88 pallets wide, 90 narrow.
  • Cooler is floor-stacked: 60 positions wide, 38 narrow.
  • Dry storage holds boxes and bags only. Growing supplies stay where they are; coir slabs stay outdoors.
  • Left out as small, under a pallet a month each: labels, chemicals, food-safety kits, bagged micronutrients, clips and twine. They go on shelving.
