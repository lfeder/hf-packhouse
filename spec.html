Placement (wide option). Packhouse north, fert building south, 50 ft apart with the 30 ft deep covered bin wash in that gap, leaving a 20 ft lane. Grade falls north to south, so the docks are on the low south part of each east wall and the at-grade doors on the high end. The packhouse docks sit in seal wedges angled about 22° toward the south, each container backing in along a 115 ft strip. Each wedge is the smallest shape that turns the seal: the face runs from the far side of the door back to the wall, so it is 10.5 ft along the wall, 4.3 ft out at the deep side and zero at the shallow side. That pulls the rigs 5.7 ft closer to the building, which is what keeps the packhouse's 115 ft apron on the property at 22°. The fert dock is square: it sits far enough south that a straight apron fits, and squaring it buys a flat dock seal instead of a wedge and a straight forklift run into dry storage.

If we keep one building instead: collapse the fert room and storage into the packhouse as
zones, push the whole envelope to food-grade spec, and accept that dry-goods and chemical semis reach the building through the crop zone.

3.3 Open items

  • Cold storage: 60 floor positions as drawn. Confirm against the finished-goods pallets it has to hold.
  • Dry storage: two high on the floor plus one shelf gives 288 positions. At +50% production, boxes and bags need about 361 (trays and film stay in the lettuce packhouse); about 3 months of stock if full. See storage.html.
  • Fert storage: 88 positions two-high is about 5 months of fertiliser at today's burn (3.8 pallets a week, the five palletised salts). If burn scales with growing area (240,000 ft² new on 110,404 ft² today) it is under 2 months; set the reorder cadence to match.
  • Angled dock seals (packhouse only): confirm the dock angle (drawn at about 22°), the wedge (drawn 10.5 ft along the wall × 4.3 ft out, an 11.3 ft face) and a turning check for a 45 ft container rig with the dock equipment vendor. Check the same turn for the square fert dock, which needs the rig lined up straight for its last 115 ft off the road loop — the drawing tests the apron rectangle, not the approach. The wedge tapers to nothing at its shallow end — expect the last 18 in to be poured as a guide curb rather than built as wall, and confirm the seal still lands with the container's corner that close to the building. The property line is traced to ±2 ft; confirm against the Pattison survey.
  • Bin wash: where the wash water drains to, and the awning height (drawn at 14 ft clear).
  • Internal forklift doors are 10 × 10 ft: cooler to pack room, and dry storage to fert room.
  • Drains in both buildings. Chemical containment in the fert room is a separate question from floor drainage and should not be answered by the same line.
  • HVAC: cool-bots for the packhouse cold room, fans for the fert building. Confirm it holds 50–55 °F. Do not spec below 50 °F, cucumbers chill-injure.
  • Ventilation: roof vents both buildings, and whether the packhouse can cross-ventilate through its doors well enough to be pleasant to work in.
  • Packhouse electrical load, once the pack line equipment schedule exists.
  • Solar on both roofs: price, tax credits, batteries on the fert building, and whether it can feed the Lettuce GH given its deficits.
  • Reconcile the packhouse sizes. Two at 8,000 ft² is 16,000 against a 17,400 ft² SUPPORT block and a 16,792 ft² J. Huete technical area. Close the gap before it reaches bid.
  • Confirm the substrate changeout plan — covered laydown or staged container drops.
  • Chemical room separation rating, once the stored chemical list is known.
  • Decide: two buildings as specced, or one combined building.
