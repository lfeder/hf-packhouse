Grades

Both floors and the pad between them sit at one level, FF 0.00, set at existing grade along the packhouse's north edge. Every dock apron drops to −4.00 at the dock face so a truck bed meets the 48 in docks, then ramps back up to the pad. The sides of each well are graded at 2:1 instead of walled, so the only retaining is the buildings' own dock walls. Elevations are in feet relative to the floor; existing grade and cut and fill come next, from the Pattison topo.

How it is set

  • One floor level for both buildings and the pad. Grade falls north to south, so setting the floor at the packhouse's north edge puts the at-grade roll-ups R1 and R2 on that wall truly at grade, keeps the forklift run from the fert building across the bin wash to R3 flat, and leaves the south part of the pad above existing ground: fill there, cut little.
  • Docks 4 ft down. The packhouse's two angled docks share one well; the fert building's container dock D1 and box-truck dock D2 each get their own. Each falls 2% over the length a truck stands in (50 ft for a container, 30 ft for a box truck) so the trailer sits nearly level and its top clears the wall, then ramps back up at 6% for containers and 8% for the box truck. The container wells run 100 ft and the box-truck well 72.5 ft.
  • Side slopes instead of retaining walls. Each well's sides fall 2:1 from the pad down into it: 8 ft wide at the dock face, running out to nothing where the ramp meets the pad. The buildings' dock walls retain the 4 ft at the face; nothing else does. Both buildings' P1 doors moved along the wall to land on level ground beside the slopes. A 3:1 slope (12 ft at the face) is easier to mow if the room is there.
  • Drainage. Each well's low point is against the building, so it gets a trench drain across the dock face, to a gravity outlet if the falls allow, otherwise a sump pump. The bin wash falls 1% away from the packhouse and the lane falls 1.5% from the fert building to a shared trench drain along the bin wash's outer edge, at −0.30.
  • Door sills. Personnel doors sit 2 to 6 in above the paving outside so water stays out; the roll-ups get a flush threshold with the paving falling away. Those few inches are left off the plan.
  • Container beds sit high. A container on a chassis can stand taller than a 48 in dock truck, and the edge-of-dock leveller only bridges about ±5 in. Confirm the 4 ft drop with the dock vendor before it is poured (Dock detail).
  • Next: existing grade. Fitting the Pattison topo (1 ft contours, 2025-12-03) to this plan gives existing grade at every spot here, and from that the cut and fill. The ramps back up to the pad assume the pad is at 0.00 all the way out; where existing grade outside falls away, the ramps shorten.
