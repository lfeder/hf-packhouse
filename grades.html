How it is set

  • Datum. Finished floor for both buildings is the average existing grade along the packhouse's north edge, so R1, R2 and P4 on that wall open at grade. Everything else is relative to it.
  • Pad, floors and lane at 0.00. The buildings, the lane and bin wash between them, a 10 ft margin round them and the area north of the packhouse (its truck aprons and the north-wall parking) are level with the floor. Grade falls to the south, so most of this is fill.
  • Truck court at −4.00 at the wall, rising 1% east over the 50 ft a trailer stands in (containers are 45 ft at most), so water runs back to a trench drain along the wall and a trailer sits tipped slightly toward its dock. Past that the tractor turns on a climb of 8% until it meets the ground, so it does not dig deep into the bank that rises toward the property line. It stops 8 ft inside the line and is held from the pad by a 4 ft retaining wall on the east wall line: the buildings' foundations and the gap between them.
  • Over the property line. Grade at the line is about 2,598 to 2,600, a bank up to the higher ground next door. Where the court's cut slope cannot finish inside the 8 ft margin it runs onto Lot 9, which the survey shows as Hawaii Farming's; that needs the owner's say-so and a grading permit that covers both lots, not a wall.
  • Trucks come in from either end. From the north at grade, past R2's apron: the court starts at 0.00 level with the packhouse's north edge and ramps down the east wall at 8% to −4.00 by 50 ft, just before the first dock, D2, so the retaining wall tapers from nothing at the corner to 4 ft at the docks. From the south almost level: there the court is only about a foot below existing ground, along the road east of Grow 3.
  • Recessed docks, the other scheme. The yard east of the buildings stays at existing grade. The packhouse's D1 and D2 share one conventional well, −4.00 at the face for the 50 ft a trailer stands in, then up at 8% to meet the yard, with wing walls down its sides. The fert building already stands about 3.5 ft above the ground on its east side, so its docks need only a trim. A short wall closes the lane's east end, and the forklift ramp runs down to the yard. Far less cut, so most of the pad's fill has to be brought in.
  • West and south sides, all options. Nothing opens on the west walls, so each building's west footing holds the step to the ground and the 20 ft strip toward the Lettuce Grow stays at natural grade for the septic. That footing is up to 4 ft high at the floor's present level (1.2 ft in option 2). On the south the pad's 10 ft margin ties down at 2:1 in the 16 ft left before Grow 1–3: an 8 ft slope at most, which fits. Lowering the floor shortens both, at the cost of more cut on the east and a north side that stands above the floor and has to drain away from the packhouse.
  • Option 2, buildings at grade. Each floor sits at the average ground under its building, 2596.2 for the packhouse and 2593.8 for the fert building, with the lane ramping about 5% between them. Almost no fill, but every dock needs its own well, and with the yard rising east toward the property line each well runs long before it meets the ground, so it needs long wing walls.
  • Forklift ramp from the lane down to the court, 10% over 40 ft, 12 ft wide, along the fert building's north wall, for unloading flatbeds parked in the court. A short wall with a rail holds the lane beside it.
  • Everything else ties back to existing ground at 2:1 (a foot of height over 2 ft across), cut or fill as the ground needs, with no other retaining walls. On the west side the pad stands up to 4 ft above the ground falling toward the Lettuce Grow, so its fill slope takes about 8 ft of the 20 ft gap the septic sits in.
  • How the volumes are worked out. Existing ground is a surface through every contour of the Pattison topo (1 ft contours, 2025-12-03), fitted to the site map on the six lot corners to within 0.3 ft (scripts/topo-site.py). Volumes are finished grade less existing on a 2 ft grid, to finished grade, with no allowance for topsoil stripping, slab base, footings or shrinkage; expect the real numbers within about 20%. The fill under the buildings is to the underside of the floor build-up, so it is a little high.
  • Container beds sit high. A container on a chassis can stand taller than a 48 in dock truck, and the edge-of-dock leveller only bridges about ±5 in. Confirm the 4 ft drop with the dock vendor (Dock detail).
